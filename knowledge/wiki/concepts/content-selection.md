# Content Selection: picking from master for a JD

Tailoring is selection. The master holds everything true about the candidate. For each JD, the agent picks the experiences, projects, bullets, and skills that are most relevant, orders them so the most relevant come first, and words them in the JD's vocabulary. Nothing here depends on the field: the JD defines what is relevant, and the master defines what is available. Read on every tailor (step 0).

## Rules for the tailoring agent

1. **Read the JD for requirements.** List its must-haves (the top requirements by position, repetition, and "required" wording), its nice-to-haves, and the exact terms it uses for skills, tools, and responsibilities. (`keywords-and-skills.md` Rule 5)
2. **Score every master item against those requirements.** For each project, each bullet, and each skill: which must-have or nice-to-have does it evidence, and how directly? Direct evidence with an outcome beats related evidence, which beats unrelated content.
3. **Select within the budget.** Fill the page from the highest-scoring content down, within the bullet caps (Hard Constraint 11) and the Content Budget. Projects and bullets that evidence nothing in the JD are the first left out. Every Experience entry stays, with its most relevant bullets (Hard Constraint 7).
4. **Order by relevance.** Within each entry, the bullet that best evidences a must-have comes first. Projects are ordered by relevance too. Entries themselves stay reverse-chronological (Hard Constraint 7).
5. **Cover the must-haves or name the gap.** Every must-have either has selected evidence or becomes a "Gaps to discuss" entry and a Gate 2 question. Never invent.
6. **Skills come from JD ∩ master.** Keep the skills the JD names and master supports, in the JD's casing, ordered by how much the JD stresses them. Drop skills the JD doesn't care about. (`keywords-and-skills.md` Rules 2, 14)
7. **Use the JD's words only where true.** Rephrase selected content in the JD's vocabulary when the fact supports it. Never relabel adjacent work as the thing the JD asks for, and never swap a tool, vendor, or model family for its equivalent. (Hard Constraint 3)
8. **Claim depth only to the evidence.** One project is not years of experience, and a simulation or a course is not production use. (Hard Constraint 3)
9. **Prefer evidence of real use.** Between two similarly relevant items, the one showing real users, deployment, or adoption wins.
10. **Show the selection in the diff.** The step 3 diff says what was selected, what was left out, and which JD requirement each kept item serves, so the user can veto choices at the gates.
