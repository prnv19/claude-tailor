# Structure: sections, summary, projects

Which sections appear, in what order, and what goes in each. Read when the diff proposes a structural change (reorder, add or drop a section, a summary) or when deciding how much space Projects get.

## Rules for the tailoring agent

### Order

1. **Master's order is the default.** Change it only on a JD signal (Rule 3) or a career pivot (Rule 4), and only as a proposed diff change, never silently. (schema rule)
2. **Order by career stage** when proposing one: recent grad or under 2 years → Education before Experience. No relevant paid work → Education, Projects, then Experience. Experienced (3+ years) → Experience first.
3. **JD signals**: a research-heavy JD may raise Education and Publications. A production or operations JD keeps Experience high.
4. **Career pivot**: when paid work doesn't cover the JD's field but a strong matching project exists, propose Projects above Experience. Projects left at the bottom get ignored.

### Header and contact

5. **Name larger, no section title above it**, then email, phone, city, LinkedIn. State location and remote or relocation preference explicitly. No street address, photo, date of birth, age, gender, or "Email:" labels.
6. **GitHub or portfolio link** only if it shows deployed, documented work. A weak profile is worse than none.
7. **Work authorization**: one short note in the contact block when it isn't obvious and matters for the market.
8. **An optional title line under the name** only when it matches the JD and doesn't overstate seniority.

### Experience and Education

9. **Experience**: reverse-chronological, paid work only (academic research roles count), internships labeled as such, one flat bullet list per entry, no sub-bullets. Bullet counts per Hard Constraint 11.
10. **Old roles** may compress to a one-line entry when space is tight, since the bottom of the page gets least attention. Every master entry still appears (Hard Constraint 7).
11. **Gaps and short stints**: year-only dates may hide intra-year gaps, a failed startup gets one line of context, dates are never stretched. (Hard Constraint 3)
12. **Education**: newest degree first, with degree, school, dates. GPA only if competitive. Relevant coursework only for early-career candidates and only when it strengthens fit. Awards carry their selectivity. No high school once there's a degree. Thesis only for recent grads, stated simply.

### Projects

13. **Projects are load-bearing** for early-career candidates and career changers, and secondary for experienced candidates whose paid work already covers the JD (then keep only the 1-2 strongest relevant ones).
14. **Project bullets follow [`bullets.md`](./bullets.md)**: specific, with what was built and why it mattered. Generic descriptions ("AI-powered todo app in React") get rewritten or cut. 1 bullet by default, 2 maximum (Hard Constraint 11).
15. **Writing samples** (blog, papers): the 2-3 best, easy to find, never a bare blog URL.

### Optional sections

16. **Summary: default none.** Propose one only when the candidate has a named-brand employer, a headline-worthy result, signal scattered across roles that a skimmer won't assemble, or a JD that asks for one, and only through the diff and Gate 3.
17. **If a summary is approved**: two XYZ bullets, or 2 factual sentences (role + years + specialty + domain from real employers). Never "passionate", "hardworking", "results-driven", or any interchangeable AI-sounding prose.
18. **Awards and leadership**: only with 2-3 strong items, each with its selectivity. Drop school awards 10+ years after graduation. Never filler like "employee of the week".
19. **Interests and activities**: only for distinctive signal (a competitive ranking, a major open-source role). First section cut on overflow.
20. **Publications**: keep when the JD values research, otherwise an early cut under space pressure. (schema rule)

## Disagreements

- **Summary presence**: one recruiter defaults to none (or two bullets), a hiring-committee chair says always write 2-3 sentences. Resolved: default none, propose under Rule 16. The shape for experienced candidates stays open (`needs-review`).
- **Experience vs Education first**: the same guide gives both. Resolved by career stage (Rule 2).
- **Projects position**: one source puts them after Education. Resolved: master's order, pivot rule aside (Rules 1, 4).
