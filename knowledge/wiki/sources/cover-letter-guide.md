# Cover Letter Guide

> **Raw source**: `../../raw/cover-letter-guide.md`
> **Author / origin**: A resume and cover-letter reviewer with about 10 years at Kickresume (8M+ users).
> **Date published / accessed**: Added 2026-07-06 alongside the `/cover` workflow.
> **URL**: not recorded (pasted text).

## Key highlights
1. **Most recruiters don't read cover letters**, so letters are an on-request deliverable. When they are read, most fail by being generic or trying too hard.
2. **JD-first**: the "What you'll do" section reveals the top 3 pain points. Address those directly, not the requirements list.
3. **The 60-70% rule**: apply when meeting 60-70% of must-haves. A letter that shows understanding of the job can outweigh missing requirements.
4. **Two-column mapping**: their needs on the left, your evidence on the right. Connect the dots, don't reinvent yourself.
5. **Speak their dialect**: mirror the JD's vocabulary.
6. **10 minutes of company research** (About Us values, recent projects) for a real hook. Generic praise ("leader in the industry") is forbidden, and the fallback is the role itself.
7. **Four-paragraph template**: who you are + why your background solves their problem, 2 achievements with numbers, what excites you about the company, thanks.
8. **Complement, don't repeat the resume**: give context and "why should they care", never a list of jobs.
9. **Underqualified?** Be honest, and frame growth with a concrete recent example.
10. **Read-aloud test**: if a sentence sounds stiff or robotic, rewrite it in a natural voice.
