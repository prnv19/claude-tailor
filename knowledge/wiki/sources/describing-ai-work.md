# Describing AI Work

> **Raw source**: `../../raw/describing-ai-work.md`
> **Author / origin**: Nate (Nate's Newsletter, Substack). Author has reviewed thousands of post-GPT resumes and writes a daily TikTok audience on hiring.
> **Date published / accessed**: Published 2025-12-05; ingested 2026-05-19.
> **URL**: https://natesnewsletter.substack.com/p/the-ai-resume-survival-guide-for-72f

## Key highlights
1. The first source in the wiki dedicated to **how to describe AI/GenAI work credibly on a resume in 2025+** — when every resume claims AI fluency and recruiters have stopped trusting the claim.
2. Three core moves: (1) make AI work **legible** through specificity, (2) **use AI to draft** without overpolishing/overclaiming, (3) **survive AI detectors** (GPTZero) by sounding human.
3. Codifies a **9-category taxonomy of weak AI resume bullets** — the most useful "what to detect and rewrite" reference in the wiki.
4. Codifies a **5-point AI-bullet audit checklist** repeated across all 5 roles: (a) name the model/tool, (b) describe what the AI *actually did*, (c) ground in product value, (d) measurable outcome, (e) show judgment.
5. Per-role playbooks for **PM, Engineer, Designer, Ops, Generalist** — bad bullets, rewrites, project ideas, audit checklist. The Engineer section maps to the wiki's MLE/GenAI role pages; PM/Designer/Ops/Generalist absorbed inline below.
6. Surfaces the **anti-AI-detection** vector: perplexity + burstiness mechanics, the "safe verb" trap, the "vary your sentence rhythm" prescription.

## Unique contributions
- Only source so far that names the **9 failure modes** of AI resume bullets explicitly: Vague Observer, Tool-Dumper, Inflated Generalist, Undersold Real Work, Generic Contribution, Buzzword Blender, Over-Owner, Disconnected Win, Prompt-as-Product Illusion.
- Only source so far that addresses **AI-detection tools (GPTZero)** as a hiring filter and prescribes concrete countermeasures.
- Only source so far that calls out **safe AI-tone verbs to avoid** (`leveraged`, `utilized`, `developed`, `enhanced`) and **tactile alternatives** (`rebuilt`, `debugged`, `restructured`, `scrapped`, `retrained`).
- Only source so far that emphasizes **naming the specific model version** (`GPT-4.1`, `Claude 3.5/3.7`, `Gemini 2.5`) — not just the model family.
- Provides 40+ before/after bullet rewrites across 5 roles — useful as a calibration corpus.

## Key claims (with section anchors)
- **§1** "If you can't describe your AI work clearly, it doesn't exist." Specificity is the differentiator now that AI is on every resume.
- **§2 (Categories 1–9)**: The 9-category mistake taxonomy. Each category has 2–4 example bullets + a "why it fails" diagnosis.
  - **Cat 1 Vague Observer**: curiosity ≠ experience. Belongs in cover letter at best, often nowhere.
  - **Cat 2 Tool-Dumper**: tool lists aren't accomplishments.
  - **Cat 3 Inflated Generalist**: overclaiming language for small-scope work.
  - **Cat 4 Undersold Real Work**: most tragic — good projects described too generically to surface.
  - **Cat 5 Generic Contribution**: "Collaborated"/"Worked with team" as weasel words.
  - **Cat 6 Buzzword Blender**: jargon-stacking that says nothing.
  - **Cat 7 Over-Owner**: implying you did all of what was actually team work — breaks trust in interview.
  - **Cat 8 Disconnected Win**: outcome described, but AI's role in achieving it is murky.
  - **Cat 9 Prompt-as-Product Illusion**: framing a one-off ChatGPT prompt as a software release.
- **§3 "How to Use AI Productively"**: Three wrong-ways (overgeneralizing, overpolishing, overclaiming) + four right-ways (start with real substance → very specific instructions → benchmark alternate framings → use as reflective tool). "Keep the voice yours."
- **§4 PM playbook**: Strong bullets name tool + task + outcome + judgment. 5 project ideas: Internal Support Synthesizer, Feature Prioritization Bot, UX Microcopy Rewriter, AI Evaluation Framework, AI Risk Memo. PM 5-point checklist.
- **§5 Engineer playbook**: Engineering AI fluency = system-level thinking (evals, retries, fallbacks, hallucination guardrails, monitoring). 4 project ideas: Hybrid RAG Chatbot, Eval Framework for GPT Output, Function-Calling Action Agent, Latent Chain Debugger. Engineer 5-point checklist. **This is the only role section that directly maps to the wiki's MLE/GenAI role pages.**
- **§6 Designer playbook**: Design for model behavior (uncertainty, hallucination, latency). 4 project ideas: Trust UX Patterns, Agent Correction Feedback Loop, Compare-and-Choose Prompt UI, Uncertainty Legend. Absorbed inline (no Designer role page yet).
- **§7 Ops playbook**: Ops is high-leverage for AI automation (executive update generation, task intake triage, hiring scorecards, meeting note synthesis, SOP audit). Absorbed inline.
- **§8 Generalist playbook**: For pivoters/self-taught. 5 project ideas: Multi-Model Comparison memo, Workflow Rebuilder, Prompt Pattern Notebook, Public-Facing AI Tool, Applied AI Use Cases in Your Domain. Strong overlap with how the user should frame side projects.
- **§9 "AI Detectors Are Silently Killing Real Candidacies"**: GPTZero uses **perplexity** (how predictable your sentences are) + **burstiness** (variation in sentence length/rhythm). Low perplexity + low burstiness → flagged as AI. Practical checklist: never paste AI-drafted bullets directly; run final draft through GPTZero; vary sentence structure; swap "safe" verbs for tactile ones; "trust that a little imperfection builds credibility."

## Direct quotes worth reusing
- *"If you can't describe your AI work clearly, it doesn't exist."* — §1 thesis.
- *"You don't need to be an ML researcher to be valuable in this moment... but you do need to be able to describe your work—clearly, specifically, and in context."* — §1.
- *"Tool lists are not accomplishments."* — §2 Cat 2.
- *"If your bullet reads like it was generated by GPT trying to sound impressive, it's probably not helping you."* — §2 Cat 6.
- *"AI is a powerful mirror. But it will reflect back whatever tone you feed into it. Don't let it erase your personality."* — §3.
- *"Think of GPT or Claude as a smart, fast, but slightly overeager junior editor."* — §3.
- *"In 2023, it was enough to say you'd built something with GPT. In 2024, the bar is higher."* — §5.
- *"Strong AI engineering bullets don't just say 'used LangChain.' They show architecture decisions, design constraints, and measurable outcomes."* — §5.
- *"The more polished and safe your writing is, the more likely it is to be flagged."* — §9 (the AI-detection paradox).
- *"GPT loves safe verbs: leveraged, utilized, developed, enhanced. Replace them with tactile ones: rebuilt, debugged, restructured, scrapped, retrained."* — §9.
- *"In an era when everyone is claiming 'AI fluency,' the most compelling signal is simple — you still sound human."* — §9 closer.
- *"Legibility is leverage."* — §10 thesis.

## Caveats & re-ingest notes
- The article uses 2025-era model versions (`GPT-4.1`, `Claude 3.5/3.7`, `Gemini 2.5`, `Llama 4`). These will date; the *rule* — "name the specific model version, not just the family" — is what survives. The version names themselves are example-only.
- The "safe verbs" list (`leveraged`, `utilized`, `developed`, `enhanced`) and "tactile verbs" list (`rebuilt`, `debugged`, `restructured`, `scrapped`, `retrained`) are illustrative — when the Action Verbs xlsx is ingested, cross-reference and expand.
- Per-role playbooks were *intentionally* not promoted to wiki pages (the wiki is field-agnostic since 2026-10-01). If a user targets those roles, re-ingest this source against the new target.
- The two external link assets (GPTZero, Nate's audit prompt Google Doc) are not ingested; they're tools, not text.
