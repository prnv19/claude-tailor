# How ATS Filters Resumes

> **Raw source**: `../../raw/how-ats-filters-resumes.md` (research notes, includes a counterpoint article)
> **Author / origin**: Enhancv (resume-builder vendor). Interviews with 25 U.S. recruiters and HR professionals.
> **Date published / accessed**: Published 2025-11; accessed 2026-10-01.
> **URL**: https://enhancv.com/blog/does-ats-reject-resumes/

## Key highlights
1. **Most ATS setups do not auto-reject resumes** on formatting or content. 23 of 25 recruiters said theirs doesn't. The 2 exceptions had deliberately set requirement thresholds.
2. **Knockout questions are the real auto-reject.** All 25 use them (work authorization, licenses, location). They are answered on the application form, not parsed from the resume.
3. **The ATS is a searchable database.** Recruiters filter and keyword-search parsed fields. A resume that parses badly or lacks the JD's exact terms isn't rejected, it just never comes up.
4. **Volume and timing are what stop candidates**: hundreds to thousands of applicants per role, with screening often closed after the first few days. Applying within about 48-72 hours matters.
5. **AI match scores are mostly signals**: 44% have them, more than half of those ignore or disable them, and 8% treat them as decisive.
6. **What recruiters said they want**: skimmable structure, short bullets, one to two pages, relevant experience tailored to the posting, measurable achievements, no keyword stuffing.

## Unique contributions
- Only source in the wiki with **recruiter-reported ATS configuration data**, which corrects the "beat the ATS robot" framing common in older guides.
- Separates the three failure points: **knockout questions** (form answers), **search visibility** (parsing and exact terms), and **human bandwidth** (volume, timing).
- Adds **application timing** and **referrals** as levers outside the document itself.

## Key claims
- 92% (23/25) no auto-reject on formatting/content. 8% (2/25) use content thresholds (Bullhorn, BambooHR).
- 100% use knockout questions.
- 44% have AI fit scores, 8% use them decisively.
- 52% say early applications fare better, 36% say timing barely matters.
- Platforms named: Workday, iCIMS, Lever, Greenhouse, Teamtailor, Bullhorn, Phenom, Beeline, LinkedIn Recruiter.

## Direct quotes worth reusing
- *"First-come, first-served, just because I don't have the time."* (a recruiter, on volume)
- *"Make sure that you're tailoring it to the position you're applying for."*
- From the counterpoint: an ATS is *"a database recruiters search,"* and knockout questions are *"if-then rules a hiring team switched on."*

## Disagreements
- **With older sources' "ATS rejects most resumes" framing** (`ats-friendly-resume-guide.md`, `resume-as-a-product.md` describe ATS scoring as a filter). **Resolution**: both are compatible once "rejected" is read as "not surfaced by search". Formatting and keyword rules stay, for search visibility rather than robot-proofing.
- **Counterpoint article (Hiration, 2026)** agrees on the mechanism but disputes how the "92%" figure circulates online. **Resolution**: lean on the mechanism (database + knockout questions + human bandwidth), which both agree on, never on the 92% headline.

## Caveats & re-ingest notes
- Vendor research with a small sample (25 interviews). Treat the percentages as directional.
- The two auto-reject configurations show it does happen. Hard requirements a JD states plainly (years, licenses) may be enforced mechanically.
