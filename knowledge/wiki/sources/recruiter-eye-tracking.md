# Recruiter Eye Tracking

> **Raw source**: `../../raw/recruiter-eye-tracking.md` (research notes from press coverage)
> **Author / origin**: Ladders, Inc. (career site for $100k+ roles). Update of its 2012 study.
> **Date published / accessed**: Published 2018; accessed 2026-10-01.
> **URL**: https://www.hrdive.com/news/recruiters-spend-74-seconds-looking-at-your-resume/541582/

## Key highlights
1. **The first screen lasts about 7.4 seconds on average.** It's a go/no-go skim, not a read. Resumes that pass get read in full.
2. **Six fields carry the skim**: name, current title and employer, current dates, previous title and employer, previous dates, education. They must be findable instantly.
3. **F-pattern and E-pattern layouts win**: bold job titles at the left edge with bulleted accomplishments beneath.
4. **Multiple columns, clutter, long sentences, and missing headers hurt** the skim measurably.
5. **White space is functional**: a page that "draws the eye down" outperforms a dense one.

## Unique contributions
- Only source with **measured recruiter behavior** (eye tracking) instead of opinion.
- Only source naming **which fields** the skim actually reads, which is what justifies keeping titles, employers, and dates in the template's bold/right-aligned positions.
- Independent evidence against two-column layouts, on human-reading grounds rather than ATS-parsing grounds.

## Key claims
- 7.4 s average initial screen in 2018, vs about 6 s in 2012.
- Top performers: simple layout, clear section and title headers, clear font, F/E-pattern structure.
- Poor performers: cluttered layouts, little white space, multiple columns, long sentences, no section or job headers, text that "didn't flow."

## Direct quotes worth reusing
- *"Simple layouts, with clear sections and heading titles."*
- *"Cluttered layouts, a lack of white space on the page, multiple columns and long sentences."*
