## DOs

- "Tailor content to each position you apply for."
- Easy readability, with balanced white space.
- Reverse chronological order, most recent first.
- Check that formatting survives conversion to PDF.
- Know the material thoroughly, accuracy matters.

## DON'Ts

- No personal pronouns ("I").
- No abbreviations unless industry-standard.
- No narrative style, slang, or colloquialisms.
- No photos, age, or gender.
- No references unless requested.
- Don't start each line with dates.

## Language principles

- "Specific rather than general."
- "Active rather than passive."
- "Clear and direct, not embellished."
- "Easy to scan quickly."
- "Fact-based."
- Quantify results where possible.

## From the sample resumes (PDF)

- Bullets: "Begin each line with an action verb", "Quantify where possible", and "each line should be a phrase rather than a full sentence."
- Education may move to the end when experience is more relevant. A summary section may optionally open the resume.
- A Skills section may move earlier "for some roles where specific skills are vital, such as programming."
- Section order and content can flex, for example an "Additional Information" section for academic projects that moves above Experience when it is more relevant.
