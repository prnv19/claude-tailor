## Overview

- Four steps: an ATS-friendly template, well-framed content in a meaningful order, prioritization and keywords, testing with free tools.
- "In many companies, the ATS may even use certain rules to reject candidates automatically."

## Setting up an ATS-friendly resume

- **Only use Microsoft Word or Google Docs to create and edit your resume**: submit as PDF but create in Word/Docs. "It is important to ensure that the text in your resume is easily highlightable, which is a precondition for easy parsing." The more commonplace the format, the better. Reduce margins (0.5" narrow) instead of using headers or footers. Don'ts: Photoshop, graphic design tools, online resume builders, Word/Docs header and footer sections.
- **Only use standard fonts of readable sizes**: "New fonts may convert letters into special characters which are not readable by the ATS." Use Arial, Calibri, or Garamond, minimum 10pt.
- **Add sections with standard headings and ordering**: recommended order Professional summary (titled with a headline), Contact Information, Skills, Work Experience, Education (first if still in school or under 3 years of experience), Projects, optional Awards/Certifications. Caution: "Never add symbols to your headers."

## Content

- **Professional summary**: recommended. List your best selling points for the JD, then condense them into under 50 words: why you fit, active voice, action words, starting with the role noun ("Software Engineer..."). Title it with a headline under 10 words instead of "Professional Summary." Examples follow the pattern role + years + specialty + domain (e.g. full stack with Rails and PostgreSQL, e-commerce domain expertise).
- **Contact information**: must-haves are name, personal phone (never a work phone), city/state/zip, personal email (never a work email), LinkedIn. Good-to-haves: GitHub, personal site, Stack Overflow, Medium, competitive coding profiles with achievements.
- **Skills**: "[Skill summary]: [skills separated by '|']" covering languages, frameworks, databases. It suggests citing lines of code written (a noise number).
- **Work experience**: reverse chronological. "[Company], [Location] | [Title] | [MM/YYYY dates]". Accomplishments as "[Accomplishment summary]: [Action] that resulted in [quantifiable outcome]."
- **Education**: below work experience unless a recent grad or light on experience. Degree, graduation year (or expected), school, GPA only if above 3.5/4.0, key achievements.
- **Projects**: at least 2, linked to GitHub or a live view, with key contributions and scale (Docusaurus example: "Used by 7.6k projects").
- **Awards and certifications**: only job-related, quantified ("Best All-Round Product out of 50 teams").

## Optimize with keywords

- **Less is more**: "Highlighting a few of your best achievements is better than including many 'average' achievements." One page. Don't list everything to show quantity.
- **Keyword optimization**: hiring managers and recruiters scan for valued keywords first. Info callout: "Some ATS will determine the strength of your skills based on the frequency of a keyword in your resume, and others assign an estimated amount of experience for a skill based on its placement" (example: a 3-year job mentioning SEM read as 5 years of SEM).
- **Include keywords from job descriptions**: put must-have and good-to-have terms in Skills and repeat them in Experience and Education, imitating the JD's language. Include full forms of abbreviations ("Amazon Web Services instead of AWS"). No stuffing: "the resume will be read by a recruiter or hiring manager eventually."
- **Optimize keyword frequency and placement**: weight each term's frequency by its importance in the JD. Expert tip: for a role type, gather 3-5 JDs, run them through a word-frequency tool (e.g. Online-Utility.org Text Analyzer), and add the skills you have.

## Tools

1. Paid human review (affiliate, not captured).
2. ATS-style scanners for readability (Resume Worded, AI Resume Judge) and JD match (Targeted Resume, Resume Shortlister), which suggest keywords to add.
3. **The plain text file test**: paste the resume into a plain text document and fix it if points are missing, characters display incorrectly, or sections are disorganized.

## Cover letters

- The letter should "complement rather than replicate" the resume, tailored to the company's mission and the role.
- Structure: capture attention with why you're ideal, express your value, tell a brief relevant story, stay under a page.
- Pitfalls: generic letters, repeating the resume, verbosity, errors. Avoid cliches. Proofread or get a second reader.
- Its sample (an ML engineer's letter) is a four-paragraph life story: an Argentina-economics hook, background, achievements (two deals contributing over 10% of portfolio revenue), and mission alignment with a fintech.

## Final tips

- **Do not take job application forms lightly**: portal Work Experience and Education fields are often parsed and filtered directly, and the resume itself may never be seen.
- **Do not apply to many jobs at the same company**: recruiters see every application, and scattered titles (e.g. Software Engineer and Data Scientist) suggest a lack of focus or self-awareness.
