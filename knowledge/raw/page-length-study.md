## Method

- Ran October 15 to November 2, 2018.
- **482 participants** with direct hiring experience (recruiters, hiring managers, HR, C-suite).
- Hiring simulation: participants screened resumes for a range of positions and picked candidates to recommend.
- Each one-page resume had a two-page counterpart with similar credentials. One-page versions ran **350-500 words**, two-page versions **700-850 words**.

## Findings

- Recruiters were "2.3 times as likely to prefer two-page resumes over one-page resumes" (5,375 of 7,712 chosen resumes were two pages).
- By level: **1.4x** for entry-level, **2.6x** mid-level, **2.9x** managerial.
- Two-page resumes scored 21% higher on average (8.6 vs 7.1).
- Time spent: 2 min 24 s on one-page vs 4 min 5 s on two-page.

## Caveats the authors and coverage note

- A simulation, not live hiring. Participants may be "unaware of their own tendency of favoring longer resumes."
- The two-page versions also carried roughly twice the content, so the study does not isolate page count from detail.
- No guidance on when one page remains the better choice.
