> Research notes: key claims and short quotes, keeping the original's section numbers and headings. Not a copy of the original.

## Method

- Interviews in September-October 2025 with **25 U.S. recruiters and HR professionals** across tech, healthcare, finance, manufacturing, CPG, energy, education, and publishing, at companies of 120 to 50,000+ employees.
- Same question set for everyone: ATS configuration, auto-rejection, match scores, knockout filters, resume priorities, application volume, screening process.

## Findings

- **92% (23 of 25)**: their system does not auto-reject resumes on formatting or content.
- **8% (2 of 25)**: deliberately configured content-based auto-rejection (Bullhorn and BambooHR users with match thresholds or experience counts), described as job-requirement gates, "not font police rules."
- **100%** use knockout questions for eligibility (work authorization, required licenses, location).
- **44%** have AI match/fit scores available. More than half of those disable or ignore them, and only **8%** treat the score as decisive.
- Platforms named: Workday, iCIMS, Lever, Greenhouse, Teamtailor, Bullhorn, Phenom, Beeline, LinkedIn Recruiter.

## What actually stops candidates

- **Volume**: "400 or 500 resumes" for data analyst roles, "2,000 applicants" for software engineering, "130 plus…sometimes in a couple of hours."
- **Timing**: 52% say applying early helps, 36% say timing doesn't matter much. Recruiters describe stopping review once a pool is full ("first-come, first-served, just because I don't have the time").
- **Human bandwidth**, not software, ends most screens.

## What recruiters said they want

- "Clear, skimmable structure" (92% named it).
- Short bullet points instead of paragraphs (72%).
- One to two pages maximum (64%).
- Relevant experience and skills (88%), tailored naturally to the posting, no keyword stuffing.
- Achievements with measurable results (52%).
- Apply within roughly 48-72 hours of posting. 32% recommend a referral or mutual connection to get past volume.

## Counterpoint (a second analysis, 2026)

- Agrees on the mechanism: an ATS is "a database recruiters search", knockout questions are "if-then rules a hiring team switched on", and the only true auto-reject.
- Describes recruiter search as literal Boolean search over parsed fields (example: `software AND (engineer OR developer) AND (...)`). Missing the exact term means a resume is "never read," not formally rejected.
- Separates modern parsers (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby) from legacy ones with weaker parsing (Workday, Taleo, iCIMS).
- Disputes the provenance of the "92% of recruiters" statistic as it circulates online, and traces the opposite "75% of resumes are rejected by ATS" claim to an unsourced ~2012 vendor claim.
