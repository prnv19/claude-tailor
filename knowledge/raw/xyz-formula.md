## Core claim

- The resume's sole purpose is "to get you past that first screen and into an interview."
- Assumes the companion post on mistakes has been applied: the resume is already "concise, error-free, legible, and honest."
- Formula, verbatim: **"Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]"**.
- The gloss: "start with an active verb, numerically measure what you accomplished, provide a baseline for comparison, and detail what you did to achieve your goal."

## The four moves inside the formula

1. Active verb first.
2. A number for what was accomplished.
3. A **baseline or comparison** that makes the number meaningful (the step most rewrites skip).
4. How it was done, which "adds credibility and gives insight into your strengths."

## Worked examples (paraphrased pairs)

- Weak: "Studied financial performance of companies and made investment recommendations." Strong: improved portfolio performance by 12% ($1.2M) over one year by refining cost-of-capital calculations and re-weighting the portfolio.
  - Why the "$1.2M" matters: it "anticipates the reviewer's question about whether 12% is a big deal or not" (12% of $100 vs 12% of $10M).
- Weak: "Responsible for negotiating service contracts." Strong: negotiated a 30% ($500k) cost reduction, and better still, says how (an online auction of multiple vendors).
- Weak: a skills line claiming "Excellent customer service skills." Strong: the same claim "as demonstrated by" employee of the month four months running. Even a skills claim takes a proof clause.
- A multi-number example decomposes one total (30% logistics savings, $900k over five years) into its drivers (returns -8%, overtime -7%, inventory -5%, outsourcing -10%).
- A veteran's bullet left unchanged: one of three officers selected to lead a training redesign for thousands of people, raising measured proficiency 20%. Selectivity ("one of three") is itself a number.

## Closing points

- "There is almost always something you can point to that differentiates you from others", even in jobs that feel unmeasurable (a waiter example: 120% of dessert targets vs a 98% average).
- "Recruiters will nevertheless love the specificity."
- "Providing data helps. Making it meaningful with a comparison helps even more."
