> Research notes: key claims and short quotes, keeping the original's section numbers and headings. Not a copy of the original.

## Method

- Two stages: recruiters first screened resumes for speed, then an eye-tracking lab stage followed where their eyes went as they read.
- Update of the 2012 study, which found about 6 seconds.

## Headline finding

- Average **initial screen: 7.4 seconds** (2018), up from about 6 seconds in 2012.

## What recruiters looked at in those seconds

1. Name
2. Current title and employer
3. Start and end dates of the current role
4. Previous title and employer
5. Start and end dates of the previous role
6. Education

Recruiters also skim for layout, job titles, text flow, and keywords.

## Layout that worked

- "Simple layouts, with clear sections and heading titles."
- Layouts that follow **F-pattern and E-pattern** reading: bold job titles supported by bulleted lists of accomplishments.
- A clear font.

## Layout that hurt

- "Cluttered layouts, a lack of white space on the page, multiple columns and long sentences."
- Missing section or job headers.
- "Text that didn't flow or draw the eye down the page."

## Caveat

- The study's job platform served $100k+ roles, so the recruiter population skews toward mid/senior hiring. The 7.4 seconds is an initial-screen average, not total reading time: resumes that pass the skim get read in full.
